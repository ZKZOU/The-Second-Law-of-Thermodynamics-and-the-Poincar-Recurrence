# The Second Law of Thermodynamics and the Poincaré Recurrence

There is no contradiction between the law of entropy increase and the Poincaré recurrence: the law of entropy increase is the law governing the evolution of the real universe, whereas the Poincaré recurrence is posited for an idealized mathematical system—they point to different system objects.

The Poincaré recurrence belongs to a non-gradient-driven, reversible dynamical system; but as long as the evolution is driven by the gradient of the system's energy distribution, the system is a dissipative thermodynamic system (the system's energy distribution becomes increasingly uniform), and this is what truly obeys the second law of thermodynamics—the Poincaré recurrence is no longer a valid description.

So is the universe we inhabit actually an energy-gradient-driven dissipative system? Or is it a dissipationless system as characterized by Poincaré? This is precisely where the physics of the real world differs from idealized mathematical models.

Although contemporary mainstream quantum physics theories generally adopt reversible dynamics as the underlying rule of system evolution (a mathematical construct), does this postulate of time-reversal symmetry really accord with the phenomena?

One point needs to be added here:

1. Some so-called experiments claiming that spontaneous quantum processes are reversible actually rely on external lasers, magnetic controls, and other devices to input energy—they are not spontaneously reversible.

2. All atoms above absolute zero will dissipate energy through radiation.

In the real world, does an isolated system with spontaneously reversible evolution exist?
